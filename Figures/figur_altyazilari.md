# Figure captions (draft, American English)

**Figure 1.** Study design and analytic samples from the Income and Living Conditions Survey cross-sections, 2023–2025. Band heights are proportional to the number of households (left and middle) or persons (right). Income poverty (H1) was analyzed in each year separately. Targeting models (H2) were trained on the 2023 and 2024 surveys and tested on 2025 households matched to neither earlier survey by member signature or reference-person key; matched households were added back in a sensitivity test. Dietary frequency (H3) was compared among adults of the 2025 survey grouped by food insecurity and income poverty. FI, food insecure.

**Figure 2.** Food insecurity outside the income poverty line, 2023–2025. (**a**) Weighted shares of households and persons who are food insecure and poor, food insecure and not poor, or poor and food secure; values give the exclusion error of the 60% poverty line, i.e., the share of the food insecure who are not poor (95% CI). (**b**) Probability of household food insecurity by relative income (equivalized disposable income divided by the national median), 2023 and 2025, from survey-weighted logistic regression on a restricted cubic spline of log relative income (bands, 95% CI); below, the income distribution of food-insecure households in 2025 on either side of the 60% line. (**c**) Exclusion error by household type, 2025; n FI, food-insecure households. (**d**) Children living in food-insecure, non-poor households (missed by the poverty line), millions; labels give the share of children in food-insecure households who are not poor. (**e**) Erreygers concentration index of food insecurity by equivalized-income rank (persons); the index ranges from −1 to 1. Error bars, 95% CI from Taylor linearization with household and person weights and households as clusters.

**Figure 3.** Identifying food-insecure households with income and structural features, 2025 main test set. (**a**) Share of food-insecure households selected against the share of households targeted, for ranking by relative income, a rule set on structural features, LightGBM with the register-like subset, with all structural features or with income added, and the poverty line topped up by LightGBM among non-poor households; markers show fixed rules; dotted line, the poverty line's targeting rate (15.4% of households); shaded bands, 95% household-bootstrap intervals. MSD, material and social deprivation (12 items without the meat item). (**b**) Paired differences from the poverty line, at its targeting rate, in the share of food-insecure households and of children in food-insecure households that each rule selects; 95% household-bootstrap intervals (8,000 replicates). Rule set, depth-3 classification tree whose leaves are selected in order of training-year prevalence; Lasso ≤6, lasso logistic regression with at most six variables, coefficients re-estimated (relaxed lasso); Ridge, ridge logistic regression with linear terms (open marker) or cubic B-splines for continuous features; register-like, survey-measured features of the kind held in population, address, education or social-security registers; + financial strain, structural features, income and self-reported financial strain. (**c**) The structural rule set: depth-3 tree trained on 2023–2024. Leaves show the share food insecure in the 2025 test set (large) and in the training years, and the share of 2025 households (hh); at the poverty line's targeting rate the rule set takes the shaded leaves in full and part of the dashed leaf.

**Figure 4.** Feature contributions, non-linearity and interactions in the targeting model (LightGBM with income and structural features, 2025 main test set). (**a**) SHAP values of the twelve features with the largest weighted mean absolute contribution, for 4,000 households drawn with probability proportional to weight; color, feature value (gray, nominal region). (**b**) Summed contribution of the two income features (relative income and household income quintile) by relative income; line and band, weighted mean and interquartile range in income bins; shading, below the 60% line. (**c**) Reduction in log-loss relative to a ridge logistic model with linear terms, for models of increasing flexibility, with income and structural features (left) and structural features only (right): cubic B-splines for continuous features, boosted trees restricted to one feature per tree (additive), depth-2 trees (at most pairwise interactions) and unrestricted trees; open markers and dashed line, year-blocked tuning folds (2023–2024); filled markers, 2025 main test set with 95% paired household-bootstrap intervals. (**d**) The eight strongest pairwise interactions among the twelve features in (a): Friedman's H statistic as the root-mean-square interaction component on the log-odds scale (400 households drawn with probability proportional to weight).

**Figure 5.** Dietary frequency and health-cost burden by food insecurity and income poverty, 2025. (**a**) Standardized prevalence of eating meat, chicken or fish less than once a week, fruit less than once a day and vegetables or salad less than once a day among food-secure adults (G1), food-insecure non-poor adults (G2) and food-insecure poor adults (G3), standardized to all adults without income adjustment; adj. Δ, income-adjusted difference between G2 and G1 among non-poor adults, percentage points (95% CI), which therefore differs from the difference between the bars. (**b**) Standardized prevalence among food-insecure adults by equivalized income relative to the median; bands, 95% CI; numbers, adults per band; shading, below the 60% line. (**c**) Households for which medical or medicine costs were a heavy burden; adj. Δ as in (a), households. Estimates from survey-weighted logistic models with g-computation; 95% CI from household bootstrap (4,000 replicates for main estimates, 2,000 otherwise).

**Figure 6.** Simulation studies. (**a**) Optimism in the sensitivity of the LightGBM rule at the poverty line's targeting rate when a share of re-interviewed households remains undetected in the main test set (latent intraclass correlation, ICC, 0.3 or 0.6); 0%, all re-interviews detected; 35.8%, the share of the signature-only cleaned test set that the reference-person key identifies as re-interviewed. (**b**) Exclusion error of the poverty line after correcting for misclassification of the single food insecurity item over a grid of its sensitivity (Se) and specificity; dashed lines, specificity 5 percentage points lower in income quintiles 1–2; horizontal line, observed value. (**c**) Coverage of 95% household-bootstrap intervals for the exclusion error and the rule set's sensitivity and positive predictive value (PPV); dashed line, nominal 95%. Error bars, 95% Monte Carlo intervals.

**Figure S1.** Decision curves for the targeting models and fixed rules in the 2025 main test set: net benefit across threshold probabilities. Select all, every household selected; MSD ≥5, deprivation in at least five of the twelve material and social deprivation items, which come from the same battery as the food insecurity item; the steps in the rule-set curve reflect its eight leaves.

**Figure S2.** Calibration of the LightGBM model trained on 2023 in the 2023 (cross-fitted), 2024 and 2025 surveys; deciles of predicted risk among households not matched to the training year; dashed line, identity; intercept and slope from logistic calibration (Supplementary Table S12).

**Figure S3.** SHAP dependence summaries for the twelve most important features of the LightGBM model with income and structural features (2025 main test set): weighted mean SHAP value (log-odds; points or line) and interquartile range (bars or band) by feature value or category. Dashed line in (a), 60% poverty line. Region: NUTS 2 regions, labeled by code and principal province.
